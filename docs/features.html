Storage explorer for Windows 10 and 11

See what's filling your drive, and free space safely.

SpaceLens measures every folder on a drive in seconds, shows where the space went, tells you what is safe to clean and what isn't, and lets you ask Claude about it in plain words. Nothing is ever removed without your OK.

Get started

Install it in a minute

SpaceLens is one program file with everything inside it. The other PC needs nothing installed first, just 64-bit Windows 10 or 11.

Someone sent you SpaceLens.zip

  1. Right-click SpaceLens.zip and choose Extract All… Opening the zip and running the installer from inside it doesn't work.
  2. In the extracted folder, double-click Install SpaceLens.cmd.
  3. SpaceLens opens. Its shortcut is now on your desktop and in the Start menu.

Windows may say "Windows protected your PC", because the app isn't signed by a publisher it recognises. Click More info, then Run anyway.

You have the GitHub repository

  • Quickest: the ready-made zip is in the repository at dist\SpaceLens.zip. Follow the steps on the left; no building needed. On GitHub, open the file and click Download raw file.
  • From the source code: double-click run.cmd. It builds SpaceLens the first time (needs the .NET 9 SDK or Docker Desktop) and opens it. Later it opens straight away, and rebuilds by itself after any change to the code.
  • To install that build with shortcuts, open Settings › SpaceLens on this PC › Install, or run package.cmd to make a fresh zip.
Removing it: Windows Settings › Apps › SpaceLens › Uninstall. Shortcuts, the right-click entry and the program go away; your scan history and settings stay, in case you install it again.

See where the space went

Every folder, measured

Pick a drive and press Scan (or F5), or choose any folder. A full system drive takes seconds, and everything below stays fast because the whole scan is kept in memory.

The first page shows how full the drive is and where to start.

  • A bar of the whole drive, split into its biggest folders, the rest and free space (amber when less than a tenth is free).
  • Recoverable: how much low-risk cleanup is available right now.
  • Large files: how many files are over 1 GB.
  • Recent growth since your previous scan of the same drive.
  • A folder tree and a sortable table with size, type, date and cleanup risk for every item, plus a details panel with a breakdown and the largest files inside the selection.

Fast, honest scans

  • Several folders are read at the same time, so a full drive takes seconds.
  • Folders Windows won't let you read are marked No access, not guessed.
  • Shortcuts to other folders (links) aren't followed, so nothing is counted twice.
  • OneDrive files that live only in the cloud count as 0 bytes on this PC.
  • Stop a scan any time; you keep a clearly labelled partial result.

Compare scans

Every completed scan is saved on your PC (the last 12 per drive or folder). Pick two scans of the same place to see:

  • the folders that grew or shrank, only the ones that really explain the change;
  • new or larger files of 100 MB or more;
  • the net change, with Ask AI to explain.

Details for anything you select

The details panel shows a selection's size, file count, dates and cleanup risk with the reason, a breakdown of what's inside (largest folders or file types), and its largest files with a See all link.

Sizes match File Explorer (1 GB = 1,073,741,824 bytes).

Free up space safely

Every item gets a risk level and a reason

SpaceLens knows which Windows folders are caches, leftovers or system files. It labels every file and folder, and it explains why in plain words. Size alone never makes something "safe to delete".

Low Risk

Rebuilt or re-downloaded automatically.

Temp files older than two days, browser and app caches, crash dumps, installers in Downloads older than two weeks, the Recycle Bin.
Review Required

Could be fine to remove. You decide.

Large videos, downloaded archives, disk images, build folders like node_modules, big files untouched for a year, everything else.
High Risk

Shown, but never suggested.

Your profile and personal folders as a whole, app data, databases, mailboxes, virtual machines, password vaults, .git history, other users' files.
Protected

SpaceLens won't touch it.

Windows, Program Files, ProgramData, boot and recovery folders, links, Windows update leftovers, and folders on your never-touch list.

Cleanup pageStorage Goal Solver

Tell SpaceLens how much space you need, for example 20 GB. It builds the lowest-risk plan that gets there:

  • Whole low-risk groups first, largest first.
  • Then, only if needed, the biggest reviewable items, and only as many as the goal needs.
  • High-risk and protected items are never used.
  • If the goal can't be reached safely, it says so and suggests uninstalling unused apps or moving files to another drive.
Nothing happens until you confirm. You see every item in a confirmation window, and they go to the Recycle Bin, so you can restore them. Emptying the Recycle Bin is a separate step with its own warning.

Move to another drive

Right-click a big folder or file in Folders › Move to another drive… and pick a folder on another drive or USB disk. Windows' own copy window shows the progress.

The space is freed here and nothing is lost. SpaceLens checks there's enough room first and refuses to move Windows, programs or app data.

Never-touch folders

Right-click a folder › Never suggest for cleanup, or add folders in Settings › Never-touch folders.

Everything inside becomes Protected: it disappears from cleanup groups, plans and the AI's suggestions, and SpaceLens won't recycle or move it.

Duplicates

Finds files of 100 KB or more with exactly the same contents: same size, then a quick fingerprint, then a full SHA-256 check of every byte.

The oldest copy is kept by default and the others are pre-selected. System and program files are left out, and hard links aren't mistaken for copies.

Cleanup history

The History page lists everything SpaceLens sent to the Recycle Bin, emptied or moved to another drive, with how much you cleaned up this month and overall.

Remember: items in the Recycle Bin keep using space until it's emptied.

Know your PC

Programs and Windows' own space

Not everything is files you made. Two pages cover what programs and Windows itself take up.

Apps pageWhich programs are big, and which you never use

The same list as Windows Settings › Apps, sorted by size, with:

  • Size: measured from your scan when it covers the program's folder, otherwise what the installer reported. When neither knows, for example for an app on another drive, SpaceLens finds the app's folder and measures it on disk.
  • Last used: when you last started it from Start, the taskbar or File Explorer (Windows keeps this record). Programs started another way, like a game launcher, show "Not recorded".
  • A filter by name or publisher, and Which can I remove? to ask the AI.
Uninstall… asks you first, then runs the program's own uninstaller, exactly like Windows Settings does.

Ask about your storage

Your files stay private

Before the first question, SpaceLens asks your permission. Claude then sees only names, paths, sizes, dates and risk labels of the items it needs, never what's inside your files.

You can turn this off any time in Settings.

Your own API key

Paste an Anthropic API key in Settings › AI Assistant and press Test connection. It's encrypted for your Windows account and never shown again.

Questions are billed to that Anthropic account. Without a key, everything except Claude still works.

Offline answers

No key, no permission or no internet? The assistant still answers the common questions (free X GB, what's largest, is this safe, what grew, which apps) from your scan, without going online.

Works in the background

There when you need it

Low-space alertsA heads-up before the drive is full

Turn on Settings › Low-space alerts. Once a day, around noon (or soon after the PC starts if it was off), SpaceLens checks quietly in the background.

  • It alerts you when a drive has less than 5, 10, 15, 20 or 25% free (your choice).
  • It also alerts you when the system drive grew by 2 to 50 GB since the last check, and names the folder that grew most.
  • If everything is fine, you see nothing at all.
  • Check now runs the check straight away.
The alert is a small card in the corner of the screen. It closes by itself after 45 seconds unless your mouse is over it.

Safety

What SpaceLens will never do

Never removes anything on its own. Every removal shows you the full list and waits for your OK.
Never deletes permanently by surprise. Items go to the Recycle Bin; emptying it is a separate, clearly marked step.
Never touches Windows or installed programs. They're Protected; programs are removed with their own uninstaller.
Re-checks at the last moment. Even an item you picked is skipped if it's protected, high risk, or a folder hiding a database or project history.
Never sends your files anywhere. Only names, sizes and dates, only to Claude, and only with your permission.
Never lets the AI act. Claude can read your scan through read-only tools. Acting is always your click.
